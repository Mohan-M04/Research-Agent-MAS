Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks
Authors: Patrick Lewis, Ethan Perez, Aleksandrs Piktus, Fabio Petroni, Vladimir Karpukhin, Naman Goyal, Heinrich Kuttler, Mike Lewis, Wen-tau Yih, Tim Rocktäschel, Sebastian Riedel, Douwe Kiela
Year: 2020

--- Page 1 ---
Abstract & Introduction:
Large pre-trained language models have been shown to store vast amounts of factual knowledge in their parameters, and achieve state-of-the-art results when fine-tuned on downstream NLP tasks. However, their ability to access and precisely manipulate knowledge is still limited, and they often hallucinate when query knowledge is missing or outdated. In this work, we develop a general-purpose fine-tuning recipe for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — models which combine pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory for language generation. We introduce RAG architectures where the parametric memory is a pre-trained seq2seq model and the non-parametric memory is a dense vector index of Wikipedia accessed with a pre-trained neural retriever.

--- Page 2 ---
Methodology & Model Architecture:
Our RAG model leverages Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) as the non-parametric retriever and BART as the parametric generator. We explore two formulations: RAG-Sequence, which uses the same retrieved document to generate the complete target sequence; and RAG-Token, which can use different retrieved documents to generate each individual target token. The retriever computes passage relevance using dot-product similarity between query vectors and document vectors generated by dual BERT encoders. The generator conditions on both the query and the retrieved passages, producing output tokens autoregressively. Both retriever and generator are trained end-to-end without direct supervision on which document should be retrieved.

Key Findings & Experimental Results:
We evaluate RAG on a wide range of knowledge-intensive tasks. On open-domain question answering benchmarks (Natural Questions, WebQuestions, and CuratedTREC), RAG establishes new state-of-the-art results, outperforming both parametric-only models (like T5-11B) and specialized retrieve-and-extract architectures. Notably, human evaluations demonstrate that RAG produces generations that are more factual, specific, and significantly less prone to factual hallucinations than pure parametric models. Furthermore, when knowledge in Wikipedia is updated, RAG's non-parametric memory can be replaced without retraining the underlying neural network.

Limitations:
Key limitations of RAG include its high sensitivity to retriever recall: if the retriever fails to retrieve relevant passages in the top-k results, the generator is susceptible to compounding errors. Additionally, retrieving and conditioning on multiple documents substantially increases inference latency and GPU memory requirements compared to direct generation. Finally, updating large non-parametric indices periodically incurs synchronization overhead and may lead to index staleness.

Research Gaps & Future Work:
Important research gaps remain in multi-hop reasoning, where answering a single question requires synthesizing facts across disparate, non-contiguous documents. Future work points toward iterative retrieval loops, active retrieval triggering where the model decides when to fetch external knowledge, and expanding retrieval-augmented generation into structured tabular and multimodal image-text knowledge bases.
